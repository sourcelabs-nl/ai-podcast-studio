A YouTube transcript looks like rapid short turns and is not. Reference 1 has
3079 cues over 20360 words, reference 2 has 2489 over 16776: about 6.6 words a
cue in both. A cue is a caption line, so that number measures caption chunking
and says nothing about who is speaking.

Read as speech, both shows run on long single-speaker stretches. Reference 1 has
named monologues of roughly 5.5, 3.5, 3 and 2 minutes. Reference 2's typical
uninterrupted turn is 15 to 45 seconds, lengthening through its analytical half.

Our own episodes are not longer-winded than that. Episode 208 measured an expert
median of 433 characters a turn and a maximum of 601, roughly 25 to 30 seconds
spoken, against an interviewer median of 118. Shortening our turns toward the
reference is therefore not available: we are already on the short side.

What separates the two is not length but what sustains it. The reference hosts
drop a token of pure listening into a long stretch every 10 to 20 seconds and
the speaker carries on. That is [[backchannels]].

No numeric words-per-turn or seconds-per-turn statistic exists for either
reference show; the turn-rhythm reading is qualitative and the durations are the
ones the analysis named.
